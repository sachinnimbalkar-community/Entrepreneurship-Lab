🚀 Entrepreneurial Mindset Hands-on Lab

Lab duration: 4–6 hours
Team size: 3–5 people
Output: Each team identifies a real problem → validates it → creates a solution → tests it → presents it.

Lab Journey

Problem → Opportunity → Risk → Prototype → Customer Value → Ownership → Pitch

Activity	Hands-on Challenge	Participant Output
1. Solve Problems Creatively	Find 10 problems around you	Problem Canvas
2. Take Calculated Risks	Choose between different uncertain opportunities	Risk Matrix
3. Build Something	Create a quick MVP	Working Prototype
4. Find Opportunities	Convert problems into business opportunities	Opportunity Canvas
5. Create Value	Interview potential users and improve solution	Value Proposition
6. Take Ownership	Run the project like a founder	Execution Board
7. Demo Day	Present solution to a panel	3-minute Pitch
🧪 Lab 1 — Problem Hunter
Challenge

Give participants 30 minutes to walk around their environment—college, office, society, shop, home, etc.

They must identify 10 real problems.

For example:

Security guard manually maintains visitor records.

Society spends hours preparing monthly maintenance statements.

Students don't know which skills companies actually expect.

Small shops don't have an easy inventory system.

Then ask them to select one problem.

Rule

They cannot say:

"I want to build an AI chatbot."

Instead:

"What problem needs to be solved?"

This teaches problem-first thinking.

🧪 Lab 2 — Creative Problem Solving

Give each team one problem.

Ask them to generate at least 20 possible solutions in 15 minutes.

For example:

Problem

Students struggle to find internships.

Possible solutions:

WhatsApp community
Internship marketplace
AI internship matcher
College-industry portal
Skill assessment platform
Internship recommendation engine
Local company directory
AI CV evaluator
Mentor marketplace
Internship preparation simulator

Then introduce the concept:

Don't fall in love with the first idea.

🧪 Lab 3 — Opportunity Hunter 🔎

Now give them this formula:

Problem + Customer + Existing Alternative + Unmet Need = Opportunity

Ask them to answer:

Who has the problem?

How frequently does it occur?

How are they solving it today?

How much does the problem cost them?

Would they pay for a better solution?

Example:

500 students → searching for internships → WhatsApp/LinkedIn → fragmented information → potentially willing to pay for better matching.

Now they have moved from:

Problem → Opportunity

🧪 Lab 4 — Calculated Risk Game 🎲

This is where the lab becomes really interesting.

Give every team ₹10,000 virtual money.

Give them several opportunities:

Opportunity	Investment	Potential Return	Risk
A	₹2,000	₹3,000	Low
B	₹4,000	₹10,000	Medium
C	₹7,000	₹30,000	High
D	₹1,000	₹1,500	Very Low

But then give them additional information gradually.

For example:

"You have discovered that only 20% of potential customers are interested."

They can change their decision.

Learning

Entrepreneurship isn't:

Risk everything.

It is:

Take calculated risks based on available evidence.

🧪 Lab 5 — Build an MVP 💻

Now give teams 60–90 minutes.

They must build the smallest possible solution.

They can use:

Lovable
ChatGPT
Canva
Figma
Google Forms
Excel
Python
Supabase
APIs
No-code tools

For example, the student-internship team could build:

AI Internship Matcher

Input:

Skills
Education
Location
Experience
Preferred domain

Output:

Recommended internships
Skill gaps
Learning recommendations
Interview preparation

They don't need to build a production system.

They need to build:

Something a customer can actually see or use.

🧪 Lab 6 — Customer Validation 🗣️

This is one of the most important exercises.

Tell participants:

Do NOT ask: "Do you like my idea?"

Instead ask:

How do you currently solve this problem?
When did you last experience it?
How much time/money does it cost?
What is frustrating about the current solution?
What would make you switch?
Would you actually use this solution?

Each team must interview 5 potential users.

Then they come back and modify their MVP.

This creates the entrepreneurial loop:

Build → Measure → Learn → Improve

🧪 Lab 7 — Create Customer Value 💰

Give them this canvas:

Before

Customer

Who?

What problem?

Existing solution

What do they use today?

Our solution

What are we building?

Value

Why is our solution better?

Evidence

Business model

Now they understand that:

A product is not automatically valuable just because it is technically impressive.

🧪 Lab 8 — Ownership Challenge 🏆

Give each team a project board.

PROJECT: AI Internship Matcher

VISION
Help students discover relevant internships.

OWNER
Team A

TODAY
Build prototype

BLOCKERS
Need internship dataset

NEXT
Interview 5 students

DEADLINE
4 PM

Now introduce a surprise problem:

"Your API stopped working."

or

"Your customer rejected the prototype."

or

"Your developer is unavailable."

Don't give them the solution.

Ask:

What will you do now?

This develops ownership and accountability.

🧪 Lab 9 — Entrepreneurial Pitch 🎤

Each team gets exactly 3 minutes.

Slide 1 — Problem

What problem are we solving?

Slide 2 — Customer

Who has this problem?

Slide 3 — Solution

What did we build?

Slide 4 — Evidence

What did customers tell us?

Slide 5 — Business/Social Value

Why does this matter?

Slide 6 — Next Step

What will we do next?

Then 2 minutes of questions.

🏁 Final Challenge: Entrepreneurial League

You can turn the entire workshop into a competition.

Instead of simply giving marks for the final product, evaluate the journey:

Capability	Evidence
Problem solving	10 problems identified
Creativity	Multiple solutions
Opportunity recognition	Customer/problem analysis
Risk management	Risk matrix
Building	MVP
Customer value	5 interviews
Ownership	Execution board
Communication	Final pitch
Important rule

Don't award points simply for having the most impressive technology.

A simple Google Form solving a real problem can demonstrate more entrepreneurial thinking than a sophisticated AI application that nobody needs.

This makes entrepreneurship visible, measurable and experiential rather than a theoretical lecture.

🔥 Turn this into a hands-on challenge

For your Pune Management Association – ED Cell, give participants this challenge:

🇮🇳 Viksit Bharat Entrepreneur Challenge

Identify one problem that India must solve over the next 10 years. Understand the people affected, investigate how the problem is currently solved, identify an entrepreneurial opportunity, build a small prototype, validate it with users, and present your solution.

The 6 questions every team must answer

1. What is the problem?
Don't start with technology.

2. Who is affected?
Farmer? Student? MSME? Senior citizen? Manufacturer? Government? Consumer?

3. Why will this problem become more important in the future?

4. What is the existing solution?

5. What can an entrepreneur do differently?

6. What value can be created?

Then ask them to build:

Problem → Opportunity → MVP → Customer Validation → Business Model → Impact

💡 One particularly useful rule

Tell participants:

"Don't build an AI solution because AI is fashionable. Find an important problem first, then decide whether AI, robotics, IoT, software, manufacturing or a completely non-technical solution is appropriate."

That will make the exercise much closer to real entrepreneurship rather than simply another technology hackathon.
